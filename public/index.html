Cybersecurity Education

Core concepts simplified for students and digital citizens.

Length and unpredictability beat complexity every time. For years, services forced users to create short passwords packed with symbols (like P@ss1!). Attackers crack these in seconds because humans choose predictable patterns (capital letter first, exclamation mark at the end).

A longer passphrase composed of random words (e.g. correct horse battery staple) creates exponential mathematical combinations that defy modern GPU cluster attacks.

Entropy is a mathematical measurement of randomness and unpredictability, measured in bits. Think of it like rolling fair dice:

  • Each additional bit doubles the number of guesses an attacker must try: Guesses = 2^Bits.
  • < 28 bits (Very Weak): Under 268 million guesses. Cracked in milliseconds.
  • 36–59 bits (Fair): Millions of guesses. Cracked in minutes to hours offline.
  • 80+ bits (Very Strong): Over 1.2 × 10²⁴ guesses. Beyond the computing capacity of earth's supercomputers.

When you use the same password on multiple websites, your security is only as strong as the weakest website you ever registered for.

When a minor forum or shopping site gets breached, automated botnets launch Credential Stuffing attacks against email providers, banks, and cloud services using the leaked combination. If you reused the password, all your accounts fall like dominoes.

Human brains are terrible at generating and memorizing 80+ unique, 20-character random passwords. A trusted password manager generates, stores, and autofills unique cryptographic passwords for every single site. You only ever need to remember one strong master passphrase.

MFA adds a critical second layer of defense. Even if an attacker steals or guesses your password, they cannot log into your account without your physical hardware key (FIDO2/WebAuthn) or time-based authenticator app token (TOTP).

What it does: Accurately detects known dictionary words, keyboard walks, repetitions, and calculates mathematical Shannon entropy against offline fast-hash brute force models.

What it cannot do: No software can know your personal secrets (pet names, birth dates, phone numbers). If you use your dog's name, an attacker who knows you will guess it instantly, even if the tool rates it Fair. All ratings are heuristic estimates.
